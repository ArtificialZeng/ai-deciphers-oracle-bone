# Oracle Bone Script (甲骨文) Decipherment — Research Report

**Date read:** 25–26 Nov 2026 (all sources accessed 2026-11-25 unless stated).  
**Scope:** Shang dynasty, c. 1250–1046 BCE (Wikipedia infobox: c. 1250–c. 1050 BC; AlphaOracle: c. 1250–1046 BCE). User wants to crack undeciphered characters starting from the simplest, excluding already-deciphered ones, with a scholarly 考释 for any proposed reading.

## Summary

- **Counts are counting-method dependent, not a single official number.** The figures the mission asked for are all attested: ~4,600 distinct characters; ~1,500–2,000 thought decipherable / in loose use; **~1,170 fully confirmed** (form + sound + meaning, attested in later dictionaries) — Liu Zhao (ed.), *新甲骨文编* (2014), p. 1081, via HKUST. The rest — roughly 2,000–3,000 — remain undeciphered, and most are probably personal / place / clan names or dead words, not common vocabulary.
- **Prize: real, still the reference programme.** National Museum of Chinese Writing (中国文字博物馆), Anyang, launched Nov 2016: **¥100,000 per undeciphered character** deciphered and passed by the expert committee; **¥50,000 per new reading of a disputed character**. Submission is a formal 《甲骨文释读成果科学论证报告》 with two senior-expert recommendations. **Only two award rounds are verifiable: 2018 (2 items: 蠢, 阱) and Jan 2024 (5 items, incl. 徹-variant and 揚-variant). No third round was found.**
- **There is no published list of "the 5–10 simplest undeciphered characters."** That is itself a finding: scholars interviewed in 2019 say the easy ones were done by the early masters and the remainder are "hard bones" (硬骨头). I therefore do **not** invent Heji numbers or readings. Section 6 gives 8 entries that *are* verifiable: 4 already-awarded cases (to exclude, as benchmarks), 1 disputed case (勞), and 3 image-only AI candidate sets plus 2 concrete newly-found glyphs with full inscriptions (礻+升, 阝+心) and the "spring" glyph — the only specific undeciphered glyphs I could verify with context and a tentative proposal in the open literature. For each, frequency / Heji number is stated as **not found / not given in the source** where that is the case.
- **AI has not verified a single new decipherment on its own (2020–2026).** Best simulated results are on *withheld deciphered* characters (e.g. AlphaOracle top-1 23.1%; CipherOBS top-10 54.3%). AI outputs are candidate lists / conjectures for expert adjudication. The one headline AI case, 勞, is a *disputed* reading, not a new undecipherment, and the paper itself says it does not adjudicate.

**Why so many remain, and what they mostly are:** Nankai 2019, interviewing Zhu Yanmin (Nankai) and Yang Xiaolei (Tianjin Normal): "绝大多数未考释出来的攻坚克难的单字大多属于死文字，名词居多" — most un-deciphered hard cases are dead words, mostly nouns, and do not block reading a text as a whole. Smithsonian (2017), summarising SCMP: "Many of the characters on the bones represent the names of people and places, but those references are lost to history" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/museum-offers-15000-letter-deciphering-oracle-bones-180964213/?no-ist . The same Nankai piece adds the important exception: some *repeated key words* — e.g. a verb for a kind of sacrifice or a warfare action — would, if read, unlock a whole batch of related divinations. Those, not proper names, are the high-value targets.

**Caution on the numbers:** there is no official fixed count; counts vary by whether variant forms are merged, whether a graph is counted as a "character" at all, and whether "deciphered" means a plausible modern equivalent or a fully confirmed form+sound+meaning+usage. Always state which definition is used. The HUST-OBC dataset illustrates the inflation: it has **9,411 "undeciphered categories"** for 62,989 images — far more than ~3,000 undeciphered characters — because categories there include variant forms / source-specific splittings, not deduplicated characters (see §4).

A "simplest" strategy aimed at the prize should therefore include disputed/variant graphs, not only never-read graphs — but those are also the cases where the evidentiary bar is highest, because a prior reading must be refuted.

### 3. Standard scholarly methods for 考释, and the evidentiary bar

There is no single codified checklist, but the literature converges on the following. The clearest compact statement is in the preface to Chen Ji, *甲骨文字形字典* (2004), and in Jiang Yubin's 2024 interview; the AI papers (AlphaOracle, OracleFusion) explicitly model the same workflow in computational form.

**Methods (in the order a 考释 normally proceeds):**

1. **Assemble all variant forms (字形).** A character may have dozens of forms; collect every attestation, distinguish real variants from carving damage / rubbing artefacts, and check the original bone, not only the rubbing (Nankai 2019: a "上" seen on a rubbing turned out on the original to be a crack). Establish the graph's structure and stroke inventory before guessing a meaning.
2. **Shape / pictographic analysis (象形, 形训).** What object, body part, action or scene does the graph depict? E.g. 虎 'tiger' as animal profile, 目 'eye', 雨 'rain' as cloud + droplets. Warning from Boltz (via Wikipedia): by the Shang most graphs are already so conventionalised / simplified that the pictograph is *not* immediately apparent (e.g. 豕 'swine' vs 犬 'dog' cannot be told apart by looking). Pictographic resemblance alone is never sufficient.
3. **Component / radical analysis (偏旁分析, 会意/形声结构).** Decompose into components; identify semantic classifier vs phonetic element; track which components are added, dropped or replaced across variants. The Chen Ji preface: "分析甲骨文的偏旁、点划、结构，合以声韵训话" — analyse radicals, strokes and structure, combined with phonology and glossing. Source: https://archive.org/download/chinese-paleography-collection/%EF%BC%88%E7%B0%A1%E9%AB%94%E6%9B%B8%EF%BC%89%E7%94%B2%E9%AA%A8%E6%96%87%E5%AD%97%E5%BD%A2%E5%AD%97%E5%85%B8_text.pdf
4. **Diachronic chain — bronze inscriptions and seal script descendants (字形演变).** Trace the graph forward through Shang/Western Zhou bronze inscriptions (金文), then seal script (篆书), clerical and regular script, and check *Shuowen Jiezi* (许慎) — while remembering *Shuowen* can be wrong (the classic example: *Shuowen* says 伐 is "man holding a ge", the oracle form shows a man being beheaded by a ge). If a graph has no descendant, that must be stated, not papered over. EVOBC was built precisely to make this chain machine-readable across six periods (see §4).
5. **Phonetic evidence (声训 / 形声 / 通假 / 假借).** Identify a possible phonetic component; reconstruct Old Chinese sound (Baxter–Sagart / Zhengzhang systems); test whether the proposed word is phonetically compatible, and whether the graph could be a phonetic loan (假借) for a homophonous / near-homophonous word. Many oracle graphs are loangraphs (11% in the Wikipedia structural survey) or phono-semantic compounds (27%). A reading that fits the shape but violates Old Chinese phonology fails.
6. **Context in divination formulas (辞例 / 文例).** Oracle inscriptions are highly formulaic: preface (date + diviner), charge (命辞), prognostication, verification. Slot analysis is decisive: what grammatical slot does the graph occupy (verb? object? place? person? time?), what verbs/nouns co-occur, does it appear in positive *and* negative paired charges (e.g. "王其… / 王勿…")? AlphaOracle formalises this as "contextual alignment" with a BERT model trained on oracle sentences; OracleFusion likewise stresses context after glyph analysis — https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21101v1
7. **Collocation / distribution (分布, 共现).** Concordance all occurrences: period (Wu Ding vs later kings), diviner group, co-occurring diviners / ancestors / places / sacrifice types, frequency, and whether the graph is always in one formula or several. A reading must work in *every* attestation, or the exceptions must be explained (e.g. as a different word written with the same graph, or a variant).
8. **Variant forms and joins (异体, 缀合).** Check whether the graph is a variant of an already-deciphered character (common: extra dots, an added component, rotation — a graph inverted horizontally is generally the same word) and whether newly joined fragments (缀合) supply the missing context. Also check the *殷墟卜辞综类* (Shima Kunio) component index and the *甲骨文编 / 新甲骨文编* for prior scholarship — a "new" reading that ignores a 1980s proposal will not survive review.
9. **Transmitted literature (传世文献) and archaeology.** Does the proposed word / rite / place appear in *Shijing, Shangshu, Yijing*, bronze inscriptions, or later lexica? Does the material culture support it (e.g. a vessel type, a sacrificial practice)? AlphaOracle's third stage is exactly this "philological grounding" against 25 transmitted texts + 23,755 modern studies.

**Accepted evidentiary bar — what counts as "deciphered":**

- Jiang Yubin (2024 interview, via Youth Daily): a complete 考释 must establish **形、音、义、用 — form, sound, meaning, and usage** — and "做到对一个古文字的'完全考释'，是极难的事情". Many graphs show only form; construction, sound, meaning and usage are unknown, and the scholar's job is to "全面搜集并分析已知信息，为考释创造条件" and to form a complete, mutually verifying evidence chain (证据链, 环环相扣，相互验证). Source: http://www.why.com.cn/epaper/webpc/qnb/images/2024-02/20/A08/20240220A08_pdf.pdf
- The prize announcement is the operational version of the same bar: a *科学论证报告* (scientific argumentation report) with rigorous argument, full materials, scientific method, standard presentation; "凡属主观臆测、缺乏科学依据的成果，一律不予参评" — subjective conjecture without scientific basis is not accepted at all.
- In practice (from the prize cases): a winning reading must (a) account for *all* variant forms, (b) give a phonologically plausible Old Chinese reading, (c) make sense in *every* formulaic context where the graph occurs, (d) explain the descendant (or its absence) in bronze/seal script, and (e) survive expert-committee scrutiny and a 7-day public notice. A single resemblance — shape *or* sound *or* one context — is a hypothesis, not a decipherment. AlphaOracle's authors make the same point computationally: shape-only outputs "can look plausible and still be unusable, because an epigrapher's judgement rests on a chain of corroboration rather than a single resemblance" (BrightSurf summary of *The Innovation* paper: https://www.brightsurf.com/news/80E0YDJ8/an-ai-that-reads-oracle-bones-the-way-scholars-do-alphaoracle-traces-form-context-and-classical-texts-to-3000-year-old-characters.html).
- **Negative bar:** if the graph is a proper name (person / place / clan / ancestor), a modern-character equivalent may be *in principle* unrecoverable — the name's referent is lost — and the honest conclusion is "place/personal name, reading unknown", not a forced modern character. That is a publishable result, but it does not win the ¥100,000 prize (which requires a decipherment).

**Practical note:** the *transcriptions* (释文) in most databases already encode a scholar's reading; an "undeciphered" graph appears as a placeholder / image / □. To work on undeciphered graphs you must work from the *image* (rubbing / 摹本), not from the transcription text. That is why HUST-OBC (images, source-coded undeciphered folders) and YinQiWenYuan (images + transcriptions side by side) are the practical starting points, and why YOD's graph-input search is valuable if you have subscription access.

### 5. Recent (2020–2026) AI / computational work — what it achieved, what it did NOT

**What it achieved (all evaluated on withheld *deciphered* characters unless stated):**

- **OBSD (Guan et al., ACL 2024 Best Paper).** Conditional diffusion model that generates a modern-character image from an oracle-bone image, with Localized Structural Sampling. Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00684v3 ; code: https://github.com/guanhaisu/OBSD . It was the first diffusion approach to beat GAN baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN) on this task, and it produces *multiple* candidate outputs per glyph (10 per glyph in Appendix Figs 10–13 for genuine undeciphered glyphs). **It did not verify any new decipherment** — the genuine-undeciphered outputs are presented as images with no scholarly verdict.
- **AlphaOracle (Liu et al., *The Innovation* 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.xinn.2026.101462).** First system to explicitly emulate the expert workflow in four stages (rubbing parsing → radical-based morphology + diachronic modelling → contextual retrieval/alignment → philological grounding). Corpus: 73,883 hand-traced rubbings, 45,364 scans, 23,755 studies. On 88 withheld characters: **top-1 23.1%** (vs 13.1% for SR3 reimplementation); contextual recovery 56.1% vs 22.2% best LLM; candidate reranking 95.2% vs 65.4%; interpretation BLEU 0.491 / ROUGE 0.586 / METEOR 0.659. In a study of 86 specialists it cut analysis time by 64% and 78.7% rated it highly useful. Paper: https://www.cell.com/the-innovation/fulltext/S2666-6758(26)00209-2 . Headline case is the *disputed* 勞 (see §6, entry 5).
- **CipherOBS / Generative Dictionary Retrieval (Wu et al., arXiv:2604.09668v1, 2026).** Reframes decipherment as retrieval against a *synthetic* dictionary of plausible oracle-bone variants generated for modern characters. Claims prior methods achieve **<3% top-1 on unseen characters**; CipherOBS achieves **54.3% top-10 and 86.6% top-50** on unseen (withheld deciphered) characters. Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09668v1 . This is a retrieval/ranking result, not a decipherment.
- **OracleFusion (Li et al., ICCV 2025).** MLLM + semantic typography: analyses glyph structure, localises key components, and renders a semantically enriched vector glyph that preserves the original structure, to help experts *see* a possible scene/meaning. Dataset RMOBS: >20k samples, 900 deciphered characters. Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21101v1 ; code: https://github.com/lcs0215/OracleFusion . Its undeciphered results (Figs 7, 13) are visual hypotheses, not readings.
- **Human–computer GAN collaboration (npj *Heritage Science* 2026).** Pix2PixGAN + CycleGAN trained on 1,160 deciphered pairs; applied to 150 undeciphered samples; experts manually refined outputs. Full conjectural readings are in the supplementary "Summary of Conjectural Decipherment Results.pdf". Paper: https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02509-4 . The authors call the outputs *conjectural* — they are hypotheses for testing, not decipherments.
- **Datasets as infrastructure (2024–2026):** HUST-OBC, EVOBC, OBWX (§4) — the main durable contribution of AI work so far is digitisation, standardised image sets, and searchable corpora, plus Microsoft Research Asia's "Diviner" (2022) image-enhancement work reported in 2024 press (180,000 rubbings enhanced; no verified new decipherment claimed).

**What it did NOT achieve:**

- **No verified new decipherment by AI alone.** No AI paper in 2020–2026 reports a character accepted by the oracle-bone scholarly community / the National Museum of Chinese Writing expert committee as a new decipherment, and no AI-generated reading has won the ¥100,000 prize. The prize winners in 2018 and 2024 were all human philological 考释.
- **Top-1 accuracy on truly unseen characters remains low** (23.1% in the best 2026 system; <3% for the 2024 OBSD paradigm on the CipherOBS benchmark). A top-10 list that contains the right answer ~half the time is a *shortlist generator*, not a solver.
- **AI does not adjudicate.** AlphaOracle explicitly "performs no additional adjudication; instead, it synthesises pre-computed evidence … to present multiple candidate readings" and "leaves the verdict to scholars" (Cell text, § Materials and methods; BrightSurf summary). OracleFusion and the GAN paper likewise present candidates / visualisations for expert review.
- **AI is weakest exactly where the remaining characters are hardest:** hapax / low-frequency graphs, proper names with no descendant, and graphs whose word died out — because there is no training signal and no transmitted-text trace. The npj paper's own count (2,500 undeciphered) and AlphaOracle's "extreme textual sparsity" point are the same bottleneck human scholars face.

### 6. Specific undeciphered characters plausibly simplest to attack

### 7. Concrete first-step work plan

**Step 0 — Set the goal correctly.** Decide whether the target is (a) a *new* undeciphered character (¥100,000 track), (b) a *disputed* character (¥50,000 track — the 2024 winners were this type), or (c) a *variant* of a known character (also prize-eligible, 2024). (b) and (c) have more evidence to work with and a clearer falsification path. Do not aim at a proper name unless you accept the answer may be "name, reading unrecoverable".

**Step 1 — Build the screening pool (1–2 days, no philology yet).**
- Download **HUST-OBC** (https://github.com/Pengjie-W/HUST-OBC) and the **EVOBC** data; register on **殷契文渊** (https://www.jgwlbq.org.cn/home) and **Academia Sinica** (https://rub.ihp.sinica.edu.tw/~oracle/02_1.htm).
- From HUST-OBC `undeciphered/` (source-coded folders), keep only categories with **≥3 clear images** (ideally ≥5, from more than one source) and **≥2 distinct inscriptions** — discard hapax legomena and damaged singletons for a first attempt.
- Cross-check each survivor in YinQiWenYuan / Academia Sinica for its *Heji* number(s) and full inscription(s). Record the *Heji* number, the rubbing image, and the 摹本 (hand copy).

**Step 2 — Apply the screening criteria (in order).** Rank candidates by:
1. **Frequency + distribution:** occurs in ≥3 inscriptions, in more than one diviner group / period (rules out a one-off carving error).
2. **Clear formulaic slot:** always in the same slot (e.g. sacrifice verb before an ancestor; place after 于; object of a verb) — the slot constrains part of speech and semantics.
3. **Clear shape:** pictographic or with a recognisable component (a known radical: 示, 阜, 水, 木, 人, 手, etc.); few strokes, not a dense abstract cluster.
4. **A component with a known descendant:** at least one component survives in bronze / seal script, so the diachronic chain (method step 4) has an anchor.
5. **Not a probable proper name:** if the slot is "after 王其于…" or in an ancestor list, deprioritise — it is likely a place/ancestor name.
6. **AI shortlist as a tie-breaker only:** run OBSD / AlphaOracle-style candidates for the shortlist; keep a candidate only if an AI top-10 *and* a component analysis point the same way. Never let the AI list create the hypothesis on its own.
- Start with the two verified examples in §6 (#6 礻+升, #7 阝+心) as *worked examples* of the method, not as targets to re-solve without new evidence — and with the OBSD Figs 10–13 / OracleFusion Fig 13 image pools as the raw pool.

**Step 3 — Tools.**
- Concordances: *殷墟卜辞综类* (Shima), *甲骨文编 / 新甲骨文编* (Liu Zhao, ed.), *甲骨文字形字典* (Chen Ji), *甲骨文合集材料来源表* (Academia Sinica, http://xiaoxue.iis.sinica.edu.tw/obm).
- Databases: YinQiWenYuan, Academia Sinica rubbings DB, YOD if you have subscription access (for graph-input search).
- Lexica / transmitted texts: *Shuowen Jiezi*, *汉语大字典*, *甲骨文金文辞典*, CUHK 漢語多功能字庫 (https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/) for component + oracle/bronze forms of *deciphered* neighbours, Scripta Sinica / ctext.org for transmitted texts.
- Phonology: Baxter–Sagart Old Chinese reconstruction (for the sound test); check the proposed word's rhyme group (韻部) and initial.
- AI (as hypothesis generators only): OBSD (https://github.com/guanhaisu/OBSD), OracleFusion (https://github.com/lcs0215/OracleFusion), AlphaOracle paper's evidence-chain template.

**Step 4 — What a first 考释 note should contain (one page per glyph, before any claim).**
1. **Glyph:** image (rubbing + 摹本), all variant forms, *Heji* numbers, source of each image.
2. **Attestations:** full transcription of every inscription containing it, with translation of the *known* words, and the graph's slot marked.
3. **Shape analysis:** what it depicts, component by component, with parallels in deciphered graphs.
4. **Diachronic chain:** bronze / seal / clerical descendants if any; if none, say so explicitly.
5. **Phonetic hypothesis:** proposed Old Chinese reading, phonetic component, rhyme/initial compatibility, loan (假借) alternatives considered and why rejected.
6. **Contextual test:** does the reading work in *every* attestation? List counter-examples honestly.
7. **Prior scholarship:** who has proposed what, with citations — including "no prior proposal found in X, Y, Z" (a negative search result is evidence).
8. **Verdict (one of three):** (i) *proposed reading + evidence chain* (ready to draft a 《论证报告》), (ii) *probable proper name / dead word — reading unrecoverable*, or (iii) *insufficient evidence — keep as candidate, state what new material would decide it* (e.g. a new join, a bronze parallel, a second formula).
9. **What would falsify it:** one sentence. If you cannot state a falsifier, it is not yet a 考释.

**Step 5 — Validation before any public claim.** Show the note to the §3 bar: form + sound + meaning + usage all addressed; every attestation accounted for; prior proposals addressed. Only then consider drafting a prize 《论证报告》 (two senior-expert recommendations required) or submitting to a journal such as *古文字研究*.
